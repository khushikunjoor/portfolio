End-to-End Product Concept

Pivot

A sustainable shopping marketplace concept designed to make sustainable and secondhand shopping more affordable, accessible, and engaging for Gen Z and Millennial consumers.

01

Ideation & Discovery

Problem

Young consumers want products that are affordable, stylish, and sustainable, but those priorities can conflict. Sustainable options can also feel fragmented across different marketplaces and brands.

Evidence

  • Secondhand and sustainable shopping experiences are often separated.
  • Price, style, convenience, and sustainability all influence purchase decisions.
  • Consumers need more accessible ways to understand the sustainability of products.

Decision

Develop a marketplace that combines secondhand products, sustainable brands, product sustainability information, and rewards in one experience.

Outcome

Product hypothesis: sustainable shopping becomes more approachable when affordability, discovery, and sustainability information are part of the same experience.

02

Research & Validation

Research

I explored sustainable shopping behavior, resale models, circular fashion, target consumers, and existing platforms such as Depop and ThredUp.

Evidence

  • Resale platforms solve part of the affordability problem.
  • Sustainable brands provide new-product options but can carry higher prices.
  • Product transparency can help consumers make more informed choices.

Decision

Pivot should not require users to choose between sustainability, affordability, and product discovery. The experience should bring those considerations together.

Outcome

The research shaped Pivot around a broader value proposition: make better shopping decisions easier, not harder.

03

Prioritization & Roadmap

Opportunity

Pivot could eventually support marketplace transactions, AI recommendations, sustainability tracking, barcode scanning, rewards, and seller tools.

Decision

Prioritize the core marketplace experience first: discovering products, understanding listings, buying, and selling.

MVP

  • Marketplace browsing
  • Product listings
  • Buy and sell flows
  • Sustainability information
  • Basic rewards

Outcome

Features such as Ms.AI, barcode scanning, carbon tracking, advanced seller analytics, and Pivot+ become later roadmap opportunities rather than competing with the core MVP.

04

Specifications

User Need

Buyers need enough information to determine whether a product fits their style, budget, and sustainability preferences.

Requirement

A marketplace listing should provide product images, price, measurements, condition, materials, seller information, and available sustainability information.

Success Metric

Proposed: listing engagement, save rate, add-to-cart rate, and completed purchases.

Outcome

The product idea becomes a set of requirements that can be communicated to design and engineering.

05

Development & Execution

User Flow

The core experience connects discovery → product evaluation → purchase, while the seller journey connects listing creation → buyer interaction → transaction.

Product Thinking

  • Reduce unnecessary steps.
  • Make important product information easy to find.
  • Keep sustainability information understandable.
  • Separate MVP requirements from future features.

Proposed Build

Translate the prioritized requirements into design, engineering tasks, acceptance criteria, and sprint-level deliverables.

Outcome

A defined product workflow that connects user needs to concrete product requirements and execution priorities.

06

Testing & QA

Risk

A marketplace has multiple points where users can become confused or lose trust, including listing information, checkout, seller interactions, and product claims.

Test

  • Usability testing of buyer and seller flows.
  • Functional testing of marketplace actions.
  • Validation of product and sustainability information.
  • Testing of transaction edge cases.

Decision

Use observed friction and failed tasks to determine which workflows require redesign before launch.

Outcome

Planned: a feedback loop that connects usability findings directly to product changes.

07

Launch & GTM

Audience

Gen Z and Millennial shoppers interested in fashion, affordability, resale, and more sustainable consumption.

Message

Sustainable shopping does not have to mean sacrificing affordability, style, or convenience.

Channels

Proposed: short-form social content, creator partnerships, sustainable brand partnerships, referrals, and community challenges.

Outcome

A GTM approach focused on demonstrating the product's value through discovery, affordability, and community.

08

Analytics & Iteration

Metrics

Proposed: activation, product views, saves, add-to-cart rate, purchase completion, seller activity, repeat usage, and reward engagement.

Learning

Compare behavior across the buyer journey to identify where users engage, hesitate, abandon, or return.

Decision

Use behavioral data and user feedback to prioritize the next product improvement rather than adding features without evidence.

Outcome

Analyze → Learn → Prioritize → Improve → Measure becomes the product iteration loop.

What Pivot demonstrates

Pivot lets me practice the full product lifecycle: identifying a user problem, researching the market, prioritizing an MVP, translating ideas into requirements, planning execution, defining testing, thinking through GTM, and establishing a framework for measuring and improving the product.
