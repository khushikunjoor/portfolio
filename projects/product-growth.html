Growth + Experimentation

Product Growth

Using customer behavior, website analytics, and experimentation to identify friction and improve conversion.

01 Research
02 Identify Friction
03 Experiment
04 Iterate
01

Research

Problem

The team needed to better understand how prospective customers moved through the website.

Evidence

Google Analytics and Excel provided behavioral and conversion data across the customer journey.

Decision

Analyze the funnel to identify where potential customers were losing momentum.

Outcome

Identified opportunities to improve the customer journey and landing page experience.

02

Identify Friction

Evidence

Website behavior revealed areas where prospective customers were not progressing as expected.

Question

Which parts of the experience could be changed to make the value proposition clearer?

Decision

Focus optimization efforts on landing page messaging, structure, and customer flow.

Outcome

A prioritized set of opportunities for experimentation.

03

Experiment

Hypothesis

Clearer customer-focused messaging and improved page structure could increase conversion.

Action

Created new website pages and marketing campaigns and used A/B testing to compare approaches.

Measurement

Tracked click-through rates, lead conversions, and engagement.

Outcome

Landing page conversion improved by 15–20%.

04

Iterate

Learning

Customer behavior provided a stronger basis for deciding which messaging and experience changes were worth keeping.

Decision

Continue using performance data and experimentation to refine the customer journey.

Application

This approach connects product changes to measurable customer behavior rather than assumptions.

Outcome

A repeatable analyze → test → measure → improve workflow.

Product takeaway

This project strengthened my ability to use analytics and experimentation to identify friction, form hypotheses, and connect product or marketing changes to measurable outcomes.
